The problem with on-chain votes

Most on-chain voting asks you to trust a coordinator, publish your choice, or fund a fresh burner wallet through a privacy coin. Goblett asks for none of that.

a trusted coordinator votes tied to wallets burner wallets per election gas for voters

How a vote travels

Three phases, fixed at deployment. Nobody, including the person who created the election, can change the voters, candidates or schedule afterwards.

  1. 01

    Register with your wallet

    Your browser makes a secret identity and your wallet signs its public commitment. The signature carries no vote, and your wallet never needs gas or to switch networks.

    wallet signature → OHTTP → Nostr topic goblett-5042 → relayer → Semaphore group

  2. 02

    Vote from your browser, not your wallet

    A zero-knowledge proof says "I hold one of the registered identities" without saying which. It leaves under a throwaway key for any relayer to submit.

    Semaphore proof + nullifier → OHTTP → Nostr → any relayer → election contract

  3. 03

    Read the count yourself

    The tally is public on Arc by design. Each nullifier can be spent once, so each identity votes once. First past the post or ranked choice.

    tally() · winners() · readable by anyone, changeable by no one

Everything an election needs. Nothing that names a voter.

The fine print, up front

Your secret lives in your browser

It's the only way to vote. Back it up from the election page; anyone holding it can vote as you.

The tally is live and public

Counts update as votes land. Who cast them doesn't appear anywhere.

The creator funds the relayers

Gas plus a small bounty per action, in USDC. Whatever is unused returns to the creator after voting ends.

Today's OHTTP relay is ours

Independent relays would remove that last point of trust. It's the next thing on the roadmap.

Open relayer network

Run a relayer. Get paid to carry votes.

Relayers listen on Nostr, check each proof and submit it. The election refunds their gas plus a bounty. A relayer can't change a ballot without breaking its proof, so none has to be trusted.
