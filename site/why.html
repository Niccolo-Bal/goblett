Why Goblett

Fair elections have used a secret ballot since 1856. Most online votes quietly gave it up.

Goblett brings it back, and lets anyone check the count.

Why that matters

When your vote can be seen, you vote safe.

People pick the popular option, or whatever their friends, their boss or the organiser expects, instead of what they actually think.

When your vote can be seen, it can be held against you.

In a club, a student union or a DAO, everyone knows everyone. A visible "no" can cost a friendship, a role or a deal.

When the count can't be checked, the result gets argued.

A spreadsheet or a poll owner's word is easy to doubt. Close results end in "who counted this?" instead of a decision.

What Goblett changes

  1. 01

    A real secret ballot

    Nobody can see how you voted. Not the other voters, not the relayers, not even the organiser.

  2. 02

    A count anyone can check

    Every ballot is counted on Arc in public, so the result is the result. No spreadsheet to trust.

  3. 03

    One person, one vote

    Only people on the voter list can vote, each exactly once. The voters and choices are locked when it starts.

  4. 04

    Free for voters

    No gas and no crypto needed. Sign in with email or a wallet and vote from the browser.

Good to know: who registered is public (not how they voted), the running tally is visible while voting is open, and your voting key lives in your browser, so vote from the one you registered in.
