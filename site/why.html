How it Works

Who did you vote for?

With Goblett, that question has no answer. Your wallet and your ballot are kept apart from registration to the final count, so no relay, server or block explorer can connect them.

Registration

A secret held by your browser

Registering creates a random secret on your device. It is never uploaded, and it is the only key that can cast your vote.

Registration

One signature from your wallet

Your wallet signs a free message that adds a sealed fingerprint of the secret to the voter list. The list is public, but it records only who registered, never how anyone votes.

Registration

The voter list is locked

Registration closes before voting opens. Every voter proves against the same fixed list, and your wallet plays no further part.

Voting

Proof without identity

Your browser generates a zero-knowledge proof that you hold one of the secrets on the list, without revealing which. The ballot is bound to the proof and cannot be altered in transit.

Voting

One ballot per voter

Each proof carries a tag derived from your secret and this election. The contract rejects any repeat of a tag, and the tag reveals nothing about who produced it.

Delivery

No single point sees both

The ballot passes through an encrypted relay, a public message network under a single-use key, and a relayer that covers the gas. Each handles part of the journey. None can link you to your ballot.

The count

A count anyone can audit

Every ballot and the running total are published on Arc. The link between a voter and a ballot never is.

    Why it matters

    Fair elections have used a secret ballot since 1856. Most online votes quietly gave it up.

    When your vote can be seen, you vote safe.

    People pick the popular option, or whatever their friends, their boss or the organiser expects, instead of what they actually think.

    When your vote can be seen, it can be held against you.

    In a club, a student union or a DAO, everyone knows everyone. A visible "no" can cost a friendship, a role or a deal.

    When the count can't be checked, the result gets argued.

    A spreadsheet or a poll owner's word is easy to doubt. Close results end in "who counted this?" instead of a decision.

    What this doesn't hide

    Every privacy system has boundaries. These are Goblett's.

    • Timing

      A ballot reaches Arc seconds after it leaves your browser. An observer watching both your network and the chain at once could correlate the two.

    • Relay operation

      Goblett currently operates both the OHTTP relay and its gateway, which in principle allows addresses to be matched with messages. Independent operators are on the roadmap.

    • Your secret

      Anyone holding the secret stored in your browser can vote in your place. Back it up from the election page and keep it private.

    • Final ballots

      The tag that prevents double voting also means a cast ballot cannot be changed.
